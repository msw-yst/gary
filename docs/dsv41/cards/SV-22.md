# SV-22: After the 15 Nov hardware maintenance: the rebuilt test machine serves as fast as before or slightly faster (gate PASS); output identity re-referenced
Status: done (gate PASS, 0 FLAG, 0 ABORT; one pre-registered deviation) · Date: 2026-11-16
Code: in the fork's release commit (build 17, internal build `deploy17`, commit 17d274cd; the link probe used build 13s). Flags: build 17's recommended environment.

## What and why
- On 15 Nov the test machine was taken apart: a CPU swap was tried and reverted (the replacement could not see one memory channel), cards were re-seated and moved between slots, and fans and power were rewired. Before it went back into service it had to show it serves as fast as on the morning of 15 Nov, and that build 17 still gives the same output.

## Prediction
- Pre-registered: yes. Each probe has a baseline from 15 Nov morning (or earlier for the link probe), a FLAG line and an ABORT line; the gate passes with no FLAG and no ABORT. Identity: build 17's output byte-identical to its 15 Nov 15:24 output.

## Correctness
- **Identity against 15 Nov: ABORT, then re-referenced (a pre-registered deviation).** The output differed from the 6th generated token (MEASURED). The expert placement had changed: 7,172 against 7,171 experts per XTX in VRAM, every per-layer helper split different, and the device numbering changed (MEASURED from the load lines). An expert's result depends on where it runs (on an XTX, or through the helper path with its int8 rounding), so unlike placements need not give the same bits (INFERRED; QA-08).
- Within the new boot the output is deterministic: three runs identical, and the expert-counter build's identity gate PASS (MEASURED). This boot's output became the new identity reference, valid only with the same placement line.
- The CPU lane carried 0 of 122,640 expert picks, so the CPU path played no part (MEASURED).

## Timing
- 16 Nov, 01:05–01:48 UTC, after the maintenance: 4 XTX + 4 MI50 at 135 W, build 17, 2.25-bit pack; served cells from build 17's served harness, two rounds; the link probe uses build 13s, as in its 13 Nov baseline.
- What differed from the baselines: the four XTX boards sat in different slots (a different board is now the tensor-parallel lead, at x16); one XTX ran at a 294 W power cap instead of about 272 W; one XTX's vBIOS read differently after re-mounting; the bench MI50 (MI50 #5) replaced one MI50 helper, which left the machine (MEASURED from the identity checks).

## Verdict
- **The rebuilt machine is back in service and slightly faster: every gated probe passed, none flagged.** One-user decode is 3–8 % faster, several users 1.5–1.9 %, first tokens flat.
- The one thing that got slower is the lead's wait for the MI50 helpers (+0.12 to +0.17 ms a step), and it has not turned into a slower step.
- Likely drivers of the speed-up, not isolated: one more expert in XTX memory, the new lead board, and the 294 W cap (INFERRED). The 294 W cap was lost at the next reboot (17 Nov); 1-user decode on later boots was 6–8 % slower again (SV-19).

- No published run: the served harness is not published. Single-process numbers on your own cards: `tools/dsv41/bench-perms.sh`.

## Notes / caveats
- The 8K one-user gain (−8 %) is the largest move and that cell has swung before; it was not treated as the new level until repeated.
- Two rounds a cell. The baselines come from earlier days on the same machine, not from a same-session A/B.
