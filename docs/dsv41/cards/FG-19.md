# FG-19: XTX expert decode against llama.cpp (QBENCH): 1.45–1.48× slower for IQ2 at batch 1, faster from batch 4; MXFP4 1.4–2.3× slower
Status: finding · Date: 2026-11-16
Code: in Gary's release commit (release 3). The same bench and scripts as HX-33 (`ds4_iq_helper_bench --pool-file`, `tools/qbench/`), run on an RX 7900 XTX, with `QB_SLICES=1` for the tensor-parallel slice shapes.

## What and why
- The XTX half of QBENCH: the same expert bytes and shapes as HX-33, on one RX 7900 XTX (gfx1100), against upstream llama.cpp built for gfx1100. Also the tensor-parallel 1/4 slice each XTX of a 4-way group computes (IQ: 768 and 512 of the 2304 rows, by the uneven split; MXFP4: 576).

## Prediction
- Pre-registered: ours within ±25 % of llama.cpp for IQ2 at batch 1, and ours no slower than llama.cpp for MXFP4; MXFP4 30–45 µs; a 1/4 slice at least 45 % of the whole expert's time; batch 8 at most 2× batch 1; error rel_rms ≤ 1e-2, rel_max ≤ 5e-2.

## Correctness
- Ours against the CPU fp32 reference (as HX-33): rel_rms 1.1–1.2e-2 and rel_max ≤ 1.4e-2 in every cell, no non-finite value, no launch error (MEASURED). llama.cpp's error was not measured.

## Timing
- 16 Nov 11:27–11:42 UTC, one RX 7900 XTX on a second machine (not the 4 XTX + 4 MI50 test machine), nothing else on that card; peak junction 55 °C. ROCm 7.14. llama.cpp b11870 (74bca395) for gfx1100.
- GPU µs from a rocprofv3 kernel trace, wall p50 / p90 as in HX-33. GB/s against the XTX's ~960 GB/s; a plain 348 MiB read reached 895 GB/s on this card, a cold 5.8 MiB read 280 GB/s.
- castagna-veloce: no column. Its gfx1100 build failed to compile ("broadcasts are not supported on GFX10+" for a DPP broadcast; `__builtin_amdgcn_sdot2` needs dot2-insts). No porting was attempted.

## Verdict
- **IQ2 at batch 1: ours is 1.45–1.48× slower than llama.cpp on the XTX.** The expert kernels are about 1.27× slower (37.6 against 29.7 µs) and our three small launches cost about 9.6 µs against llama.cpp's 2.8.
- **IQ2 at batch 4–8: ours is faster** (0.51–0.83×). Our window path reads each expert once for all rows; llama.cpp switches to its MMQ path at batch 8 and slows (INFERRED from its kernel counts, 4 → 10 a call).
- **MXFP4: 1.4–2.3× slower at every batch** (GPU time; at batch 8 the wall-clock ratio is 1.09). llama.cpp reaches 64 % of peak on the same bytes, our generic MXFP4 path 28 %: a fast gfx1100 MXFP4 decode exists, and ours is not it.
- **Splitting an expert 4 ways barely shortens it:** a 1/4 slice still takes 52–75 % of the whole expert's GPU time, at 8–13 % of peak.
- Predictions: ours within ±25 % at batch 1 failed (1.45–1.48×); MXFP4 30–45 µs failed (70.8); slice ≥ 45 % held (52–75 %); batch 8 ≤ 2× batch 1 held on wall time (1.5–1.6×), borderline on GPU time for IQ2 (2.0–2.2×); rel_rms ≤ 1e-2 missed narrowly, rel_max held.
- The opposite of the MI50 (HX-33), where our IQ2 kernels lead both references.

## Reproduce
- `QB_SLICES=1 tools/qbench/run-card.sh OUT ours lcpp kt sum` on one XTX, with `QB_LCPP` from `tools/qbench/build-ref.sh <llama.cpp checkout> gfx1100 <dir>`.

## Notes / caveats
- One card, one run. The bench measures one live pick per call; the engine's one-token path launches all picks of a token together, so the bench overstates our fixed cost per expert by up to the ~9.6 µs of small launches (INFERRED).
- The XTX ran on a different machine from the MI50 of HX-33; kernel times do not depend on the host, the round trip (HX-34) does.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
