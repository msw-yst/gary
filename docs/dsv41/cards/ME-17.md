# ME-17: Re-evaluating 39 written-off changes against the measured stage budget: 4 explained, 6 too small to see, 6 still open, 23 not covered
Status: method (desk re-evaluation over measured stage timings; no new runs) · Date: 2026-11-21
Code: none (desk study). The stage timings it reads came from internal measurement builds `stagetime-r1` 5a5153f2, `stagetime-r2` a6f7d9d7, `stagetime-r3b` 1295092b and `hostroom-r1` 5c35eda7 (not in the fork's release commit). Flags: none.

## What and why
- Over 9–20 Nov we dropped or parked many changes because they were slower, neutral or worse, mostly with a reason that was a guess, a reading of a kernel trace (which hides GPU-side slowdowns), or nothing at all. An audit of the whole results ledger listed **39 such drop rows and 14 exactness failures** that were never root-caused, plus 16 related items found outside the ledger.
- On 21 Nov we had, for the first time, an untraced per-layer stage budget of a decode token: event marks on every card at each stage of a layer (attention, routing, the publish to the MI50s, the lead's own expert work, the join, the all-reduces), from in-process A/B runs with no tracer.
- The question: for each written-off change, how big is the stage it acted on today, is that stage on the critical path, and does that explain the result?

## Method
- **The stage budget.** Per-layer stage means for each card, from three stage-timing runs and one host-timing run (single user, frozen expert cache, drafter off, so every decode window is one row; the native MXFP4 pack; four XTX and five MI50 helpers; 2K prompt plus 1,480 or 4,000 generated tokens). A stage with nothing in it reads 4.4–4.7 µs: the cost of one mark plus one dependent-launch gap (MEASURED).
- **f0** = the fraction of layers in which the lead XTX does **not** wait at the join for the MI50s and the CPU lane. In those layers the lead's own post-publish work is on the critical path; in the others the join wait hides it. f0 was 0.664, 0.374 and 0.562 in the three runs (MEASURED).
- **The ceiling** for a change = the measured saving per call × the share of layers in which its stage is on the path × 40 layers. For example, the XTX down kernel's one-row saving of 3.08 µs × f0 × 40 = at most 0.08 ms a token.
- **Exclusions.** A few multi-second whole-process freezes (seen only with one all-reduce option loaded) were dropped token by token. A broader exclusion rule changed every off-arm figure by ≤ 2 µs a layer (MEASURED).
- **Verdicts.** EXPLAINED BY EXISTING DATA; CEILING TOO SMALL (the stage cannot give a measurable gain); STILL OPEN (the stage is big enough and on the path, so the old reason does not hold, and a named measurement would settle it); NOT COVERED (the stage is not instrumented by these runs: prompt reading, several users, drafter windows of two or more rows, the 2.25-bit pack, the live cache, contexts above ~6K, or bench-only kernel internals).
- **Labels.** The stage figures are MEASURED. Every verdict, ceiling and attribution in this card is INFERRED from them: no change was re-run for this card.

## Verdict
- **Only 4 of the 39 write-offs are explained by measurement; 6 more could never have shown a gain on this hardware; 6 are still open with a named measurement; 23 sit on stages the stage-timing runs do not instrument.**
- **The lead's own post-publish kernels are on the critical path in 37–66 % of layers.** The reasons given for the XTX down kernel (FG-13) and the dropped q8 launch (FG-08), "off the critical path", do not hold as stated; the down kernel still loses on its ceiling (≤ 0.08 ms).
- **The join wait tracks the CPU lane's load.** Every A/B whose arms produce different text can drift by milliseconds through the lane alone (INFERRED from two runs of the same build).
- The re-evaluation became the master measurement plan of 21 Nov. Its first group ran the same afternoon (GROUPA / GROUPA2, carded separately in this backfill): the CPU lane is last into the join in 79 % of waiting layers; `DS4_LP_ROUTE` is off the path (closes #15); `DS4_MX_PF` slows the CPU expert compute itself (closes #31); and two items outside the 39, the lost BF16 matvec (FG-20) and split-KV decode attention, each took about 1 ms a token off (MEASURED there).

## Notes / caveats
- Everything here is single user, drafter off, frozen cache, native pack, short context. A change that only acts on multi-row windows, prompts or several users is "not covered", not "explained".
- Attributing the join wait to the CPU lane was an inference on 21 Nov; GROUPA measured it the same day.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
