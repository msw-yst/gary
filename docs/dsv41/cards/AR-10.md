# AR-10: Why direct GPU-to-GPU writes lose the decode all-reduce: landing and fan-out, not fences or the board
Status: finding (decode keeps the host route) · Date: 2026-11-18
Code: not in the release commit of Gary, our fork of Strata (lab benches and mock-ups on an internal branch). Flags: none (benches only).

## What and why
- The push all-reduce (the P2P write-up, section 5) was correct but slower than staging through host memory: 30–60 µs a call slower, verify step +3.5 ms served. That write-up left "why" as open question 1. This card is the answer from a day of microbenchmarks on the four production XTX and one MI50.

## Prediction
- The first phase's budget (that system fences cost about 5 µs each and explain the gap) was wrong: 23 of 35 predictions OUT. The deep-dive that followed: 20 of 39 OUT.

## Correctness
- 0 wrong values in every arm of every bench (MEASURED). One mock-up without waits saw 1.59 million flag-before-data re-polls in 1 million 4-row calls, all caught by the checksum: posted-write order does not hold end to end across the cards (MEASURED).

## Timing
- 18 Nov, the test machine: 4 × RX 7900 XTX + MI50; benches with the engine stopped. T = rows a call (decode verify sizes: T1 = 20 KB, T4 = 80 KB a member).

## Verdict
- **Decode keeps the host all-reduce.** At decode sizes a write lands in host DRAM about 1 µs sooner than in a peer's VRAM (HW-15), and one card fanning out small uncached writes to three peers gets a fifth of its bandwidth. Fences, polling, the board and the I/O die are not the problem.
- **Visibility is a GPU cache property:** on gfx1100, mid-kernel peer data is visible only in uncached receive buffers, and the driver picks that per buffer (INFERRED from the kernel driver's source: buffers are mapped non-coherent unless asked otherwise; there is no in-kernel L2 invalidate on gfx11). Designs that cross a kernel boundary must wait for the producer kernel to end, not for its flag.
- **Where P2P does win: large messages.** At prompt sizes a direct reduce-scatter and all-gather is 2–2.6× faster than the host route (AR-11).

## Reproduce
- No published bench. The P2P write-up's repro section covers the stale-read probe; these timing benches are not released.

## Notes / caveats
- About 10 µs of every T1 call in the benches was launch skew between cards, hitting both routes equally; in serving, skew comes from compute imbalance between cards (INFERRED).
- Relaxed ordering is not the cause of the reordering: with it off there were more re-polls, not fewer (107,578 against 31,484) (MEASURED); it was restored and verified.

## Addendum (22 Nov)
- **Correction:** the "about 4×" cost of uncached peer buffers is not supported. The same run's direct pairs give uncached = coarse (all four cards 28.12 against 28.15 GB/s; one pair 11.51 against 11.64); the 4× ratio compared a different configuration. The "translation pressure" ratio (1.81) was a cache drain, not address translation (INFERRED in the later analysis). The fan-out finding stands (MEASURED).
