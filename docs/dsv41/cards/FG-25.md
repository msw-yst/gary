# FG-25: Where a native decode token goes now: attention is 46 % and flat with context; the CPU lane is down to 4.5 %
Status: finding · Date: 2026-11-20
Code: not in the release commit of Gary, our fork of Strata (internal branch only). Measurement: the engine's own stage timers (`DS4_PROF`), one-shot CLI, on STACK's configuration (SV-18).

## What and why
- After the prompt work (PF-15 to PF-20) and the fifth helper (HX-37), the earlier decode breakdowns (FG-15, FG-17, FG-21) no longer describe the engine. This run times every stage again to pick the next targets.

## Prediction
- None (a measurement).

## Correctness
- Timer overhead measured against plain runs: decode × 1.118 (22.2 against 19.8 ms a token), prompt and batched steps about × 1.00 (MEASURED). Shares below are from the timed runs.

## Timing
- 20 Nov, the test machine: 4 × RX 7900 XTX (tensor-parallel 4) and 5 × MI50 32 GB helpers at 135 W; native MXFP4 pack. One user, drafter off, locked placement; per-XTX stage times averaged over the four members.

## Verdict
- **Attention is the biggest decode lever:** 10.2 ms a token, the same at 2K and 8K, so it is fixed overhead in the attention path, not KV reads. The desk analysis of what it is made of is FG-26.
- **The CPU lane is no longer the bound it was** (a quarter of a step in FG-22, 4.5 % here): this is why a smaller all-3-bit pack to empty it was shelved (QA-15).
- Other targets, in order: expert streaming in long prompts (4 s per XTX at 14K), the all-reduces (about 2.6 s per XTX at 14K, 3.7 ms a decode token with the down projection).

## Anomalies (reported, not used)
- **The live-cache arm read 390 ms a token.** That is not a speed: its average includes token 1, about 70 s while the cache moved 450 experts from file-backed pages with about 8 GiB of RAM free, and one token of 22 s. Its median token was 22 ms, the same as locked. The stage timers charged the stalls to the two stages that end in a spinning all-reduce, because the other members wait inside it (MEASURED; reading INFERRED). See ME-16.
- The drafter arm recorded no drafting rounds; not used.
- An expert-input capture arm (for QA-16's output-space test) wrote 0 bytes; it has to be fixed and re-run.

## Addendum (22 Nov)
- **Update:** the CPU lane's "4.5 %" is the timer's own share. In-situ join stamps measured the lane's excess at 2.8 ms a token, about 2.8× the timer figure (MEASURED, FG-33).
