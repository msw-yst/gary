# PF-19: Native prompt reads 3.2× faster at 2K: the RAM store keeps only what the XTXs must stream, and the stream keeps its batches full
Status: kept (GO; internal native build, not in a release) · Date: 2026-11-19
Code: not in the release commit of Gary, our fork of Strata (internal branch only). Flags (branch-only, each default off): `DS4_STORE_UNHELD=1` (the RAM store keeps none of the experts the MI50 helpers hold), `DS4_PF_HELPER_PACK=1` (a helper layer's unheld experts come first in the stream order, so batches stay full), `DS4_RA_STORE_SKIP=1` (the stream's read-ahead leaves out experts the store holds); with `DS4_PF_HELPER=8192` and `DS4_PF_HELPER_ROOM=1`.

## What and why
- PF-18 found two causes for the helper's slower reads: the RAM store spent its room on the experts the MI50s hold (6,804 experts, 128.1 GB), so the experts left to stream were the cold, disk-backed ones; and every held expert cut the stream's copy batches to about two experts. A third, suspected: read-ahead pulling disk pages for experts already in RAM.
- One switch per cause, added one at a time: P (helper at 8,192 rows), S = P + store fix, K = S + full batches, R = K + read-ahead skip.

- Mechanism gates: the store holds the stream (files ≤ 5 % of streamed experts); **disk bytes a 2K read ≤ 0.40 × the baseline's** in S; at least 10 experts a batch in K and R. GO for the fastest eligible arm if 2K ≤ 0.75, decode ≤ 1.03, 8K ≤ 1.00 and 14K ≤ 1.10.

## Correctness
- Every arm CONFIG ok, 0 bad steps, placement frozen; the store fix made 0 on-demand fills of held experts (MEASURED).
- The helper is not bit-identical by design (PF-11). Text closeness to the baseline: 3 of 3 2K cells close in every helper arm (PASS) (MEASURED).
- The store fix changes only where an expert's bytes come from; a held pick reaches the CPU lane only on a helper failure, and then reads the same bytes from the files (INFERRED from the source; a CPU test of the store's cold-end rule passes 12 of 12, REPORTED).

## Timing
- 19 Nov, the test machine: 4 × RX 7900 XTX (tensor-parallel 4) and 4 × MI50 32 GB helpers at 135 W; one EPYC Zen 2 16-core host, DDR4 at 2933 (HW-13). Native MXFP4 pack. Arms P1 S1 K1 R1 B1 P2 S2 K2 R2 B2; every arm starts with the page cache dropped and the same warm-up (8 × 2K and 2 × 8K reads, placement frozen after read 8). Cells: three 2K reads (~1,657 tokens), three 8K (~6,544), one 14K (~14,000), greedy. Statistic: each arm's median, mean of the two arms.

## Verdict
- **GO (arm R).** A native 2K prompt's first token 15.2 → 4.7 s, 8K 16.7 → 7.8 s, 14K 26.2 → 13.0 s, no disk reads during a prompt, and decode about 9 % faster.
- **The store fix did most of it:** with the helper-held experts out of the store, everything the XTXs stream comes from RAM, and the disk drops out of the prompt (47.7 → 0 GB a read). Full batches then took a further third off at 2K.
- The read-ahead skip added nothing on top (× 0.997): once the store held the whole stream, the disk was already out.
- Decode got faster without being targeted: the CPU lane's store misses went to 0 (INFERRED: the store's room went to the experts the CPU lane actually uses).

## Notes / caveats
- Drift: the helper-only arm P drifted (× 0.69, a cold-cache first cell); B, S, K and R were within 0.96–1.00, inside the 0.85–1.15 bar.
- The 14K cell is one read an arm. Concurrency (more than one prompt at once) and prompts longer than 14K were not tested.
- With the store fix on, the helper's length cap (PF-13's `DS4_PF_HELPER_MAXP`) is set aside, so every read keeps the helper; past 8,192 rows a chunk would otherwise stream the helpers' experts from the files.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
