# FG-16: A decode fusion plan from the kernel trace: five items worth about 3–4 % a token; launch gaps cap fusion at about 3 ms
Status: desk (plan; items 1 and 3 measured, passed, in build 16, this release; item 2 measured and passed, in build 17, release 3) · Date: 2026-11-13 (corrected 2026-11-14)
Code: items 1 and 3 are in the fork's release commit (build 16): `DS4_XK_QA=1` and `DS4_ATTN_MERGE=3`, default off in the code, both on in build 16's recommended environment. The other items are not built.

## What and why
- FG-15's kernel trace of one-row decode on build 15 showed a fixed chain of about 24 small kernels in every plain layer on the lead XTX, and 3.07 ms of "inter-kernel gaps" a token after rescaling. If all of that could be removed, fusing kernels would be worth about a quarter of a token.
- This desk study read the trace kernel by kernel, priced each removed launch from past engine A/Bs, and ranked fusion candidates on the lead XTX and the MI50 side by milliseconds saved per day of work. Nothing was built or run for it.

## Prediction
- Pre-registered in the study, per item, with an abort line each (all INFERRED): 1. the int8 epilogue for the attention output's first GEMV (`DS4_XK_QA`, one launch a layer) −0.20 ms a one-row token (−0.10 to −0.28), 0 at B8; 2. the solo-path fusions carried into the batched attention path −0.35 ms a step at batched B1 and at B8 (−0.10 to −0.60); 3. one more folded launch in the merged attention launch (`DS4_ATTN_MERGE=3`) −0.10 (−0.04 to −0.13); 4. the attention output's finishing step inside the sparse-attention kernel −0.12 (−0.03 to −0.18); 5. the lead pushes each MI50's input into its memory instead of the MI50 reading it −0.15 at B1, −0.20 a step at B8.
- Stacked, the four one-row items: about −0.45 ms (−0.2 to −0.7), 12.62 → ~12.2 ms a token, about 3–4 %.

## Correctness
- Items 1 and 3: output identical to the flags-off build (addendum). The others: not run. Every candidate is designed to be exact (same arithmetic in the same order); each would need its own flag, default off, and the identity gate before any timing (ME-08).

## Verdict
- **The five items are worth about 3–4 % a token** (stacked one-row items about −0.45 ms); that estimate stands.
- **Correction (14 Nov, from untraced on-device timestamps, FG-17):** the study's claim that FG-15's gaps were mostly a tracer artefact was wrong. The real device gap between kernels is about 2.9 µs at every boundary measured (2.92–2.96 µs median, MEASURED in FG-17), not 0.1–1.1 µs, so the trace's 3.6 µs was only mildly inflated. About 1,070 lead kernels × ~2.9 µs ≈ 3.1 ms a token really is gap time [I]: the ceiling for fewer launches (fusion, graph-style launch) is about 3 ms a token, as FG-15 first suggested, not a few tenths. The five items take only a small part of it; the rest needs launch reduction on a larger scale.
- **Second correction (FG-17):** at one row the MI50 helpers finish after the lead reaches its join in 98.2 % of layers, not about half (MEASURED in FG-17). The helper chain (~71 µs a layer: wake 5.7, head 18, gate_up 26, q8 + down 19, combine + flag 8 µs) is on the critical path almost everywhere, so every µs taken off it is worth about 40 µs a token [I]. MI50-side items (item 5 and the helper chain) rank higher than this study put them.
- **Item 2 is the one that matters in serving:** about 95 % of served decode steps are batched, and the batched attention path lacks the fusions the one-row path already has. One-row-only items are worth about 5 % of their B1 gain in production.
- **Item 2 measured (14 Nov, `DS4_BT_FUSE=1`, in build 17, release 3):** output identical everywhere (batch test B = 1 / 2 / 8 against solo; served greedy at 4 concurrent, 8 of 8 twice). Batched step B = 1: 14.48 → 13.61 ms (**−0.88 ms, −6.0 %**); B = 8: 38.13 → 35.90 ms (**−2.22 ms, −5.8 %**), about 2.5 × the central prediction. Served (reported only): 4 users 153.5 → 159.7 tok/s (+4.0 %), 8 users 188.5 → 200.5 tok/s (+6.4 %); one user unchanged (it takes the solo path). All MEASURED; see [UPDATE-2026-11-14.md](../UPDATE-2026-11-14.md).

## Addendum (2026-11-14): items 1 and 3 measured (both now in build 16)
- **Item 1, `DS4_XK_QA`:** B1, 64-token prompt, drafter off, 4 rotated rounds, four production helpers: OFF 12.975 / ON 12.768 / placebo 12.968 ms a token, **−0.208 ms** (predicted −0.20, range −0.10 to −0.28: PASS), placebo −0.008. All 16 identity checks were IDENTICAL: the build with the flag off, on and with the indexer dispatch against build 15 (and the batch test with the flag on), and every timing arm against its round's OFF (MEASURED, fusion1-ab.txt). The same run re-checked the indexer dispatch (FG-12) with four helpers: **−0.165 ms** (MEASURED).
- **Item 3, `DS4_ATTN_MERGE=3`:** same method, on top of item 1: OFF (=2) 12.951 / ON 12.795 / placebo 12.944 ms, **−0.157 ms** (predicted −0.10, range −0.04 to −0.13: PASS, beyond the range on the good side), placebo −0.007; identical to the item-1 build with the drafter off and on (MEASURED, fusion3-ab.txt).
- Together, measured inside build 16 (ablation, 14 Nov, one run an arm, noise 0.04 ms): switching off item 1 costs +0.16 ms a token, item 3 (back to `=2`) +0.15 ms and the indexer dispatch +0.17 ms; all five of build 16's changes off cost +0.44 ms (12.47 → 12.89 ms, −3.4 % for all five) (MEASURED). So they hold their gains together. All three are one-row paths (item 2 is the batched one). All three are in build 16, the build in this release (SV-16).

## Notes / caveats
- Launch-count models have come in at 0.4–0.7 × of their prediction in the past; item 1 has the best-calibrated analogue (FG-08's launch fold came in at 1.3 × its bench). Items 1 and 3 came in at 1.0 × and 1.6 × of their central predictions.
- Item 5 needs peer access between XTX and MI50 across the board's root complexes, untested here.
