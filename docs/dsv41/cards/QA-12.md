# QA-12: Mixed precision: native MXFP4 for the hottest experts on top of the 2.25-bit pack closes about 60 % of the quality gap at 1.18× build 17's time a token
Status: finding (code not in this release) · Date: 2026-11-17
Code: not in the fork's release commit (internal branch: mixed per-expert precision placement and its pack tools). Flags: `DS4_MIXQ` (branch-only).

## What and why
- QA-11 showed that the 2.25-bit weights, not the engine, are the distance to the hosted model, and that native MXFP4 experts close 88 % of it; but native weights do not fit our cards, so a native build is slow (experts on the CPU lane) and its first token is long.
- A mixed pack keeps the 2.25-bit pack and adds a native MXFP4 copy of the most-used experts, chosen by how often agent traffic picked them, held on the XTX cards; every expert stays on a GPU. Every non-expert tensor comes from the 2.25-bit pack.
- Two packs: **x10** (about 10 % of the routed experts native) and **x25c** (2,960 experts native, about 19 %).

## Prediction
- Quality bar set before the run: KL from hosted ≤ 0.019 nats a token.

## Correctness
- The mixed-precision build with no manifest, against the native build, teacher-forced at equal expert placement: BITWISE (MEASURED). (An earlier run compared them at different placements and could not decide; it was repeated with placement matched, QA-08.)
- Top-1 agreement for build 17 and native on the same prompts: QA-11 (90.3 % and 96.0 %).

## Timing
- 17 Nov, the 4 XTX + 4 MI50 test machine. Quality: QA-11's method, 12 prompts teacher-forced along the hosted model's greedy continuations. Speed: served, 1 user 2K and 4 users.

## Verdict
- **Mixing works:** x25c closes about 60 % of the gap between the 2.25-bit pack and native ((0.0438 − 0.0210) / (0.0438 − 0.0054)) at 1.18× build 17's time a token, and keeps build 17's first token (7× faster than native here). It misses the 0.019 bar by 0.002.
- **The share of native experts matters, not which ones (INFERRED, mean KL only).** The two packs' KL values fall on a straight line in the share of decode picks that hit a native expert, and that line meets native's own KL at a share of 1. A desk study of the pick counters found that choosing experts differently at the same budget (by gate weight, one global list, per-layer floors) moves the held-out share very little, because the gap is between tasks, not in the scoring. So more quality needs a larger native share, not a better list. This rests on two measured packs and on mean KL only; per-position quality and task outcomes were not measured for the mixed packs.
- Next: a larger native share, with the MI50s holding native experts too.

## Reproduce
- Not reproducible from the release: the mixed-precision code and pack tools are on an internal branch. The quality method is `validation/hostlp/` (QA-11).

## Notes / caveats
- One run a cell. The native first token is with the build's defaults; the short-prompt window path (`DS4_CHUNK_MIN`) roughly halves it on native (measured separately, 2K 18.5 → 8.85 s) and was not used in this comparison.
- The hot-expert lists come from agent traffic; the 12 quality prompts are different text.
- KL is over hosted's top-20 plus a rest bucket, as in QA-11.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
