# DR-15: DSpine and DFlash as drafter alternatives: two desk studies and two tests on another model; not usable on DeepSeek V4.1 today
Status: desk (no V4.1 drafter of either kind exists; not pursued) · Date: 2026-11-14
Code: none (desk study). The two tests ran a public llama.cpp fork with DSpine support on a different model; none of it is in our engine. Flags: none.

## What and why
- Our drafter is DSpark, DeepSeek's own drafter, shipped with the V4.1 checkpoint (DR-01). On 14 Nov we looked at two newer drafter designs to see whether either could replace it.
- **DSpine** (a paper published in October): a block drafter in the style of DFlash (5 layers, block 16) in which each position passes its predecessor's guess forward at every layer.
- **DFlash**: a published DFlash drafter for DeepSeek V4 Flash (not V4.1).
- We wanted to know: could either run on our model, and what would it be worth if it did?

## Prediction
- None was written down before the desk studies. After the DSpine desk study, we estimated a V4.1 DSpine at its best depth (4–6) at **+5 to +24 %** over DSpark at depth 3. The low end is the paper's gain over DSpark; the high end uses the acceptance curve from the 3090 test below (INFERRED). Pre-registered: no.

## Correctness
- Not applicable: nothing ran on our engine. The 3090 test's placebo (a repeat on a fresh server) matched to 0.1 tok/s. CUDA is deterministic there, so the placebo says nothing about noise (REPORTED).
- The 3090 test did not check output identity against the no-drafter run at temperature 0 (REPORTED, listed as pending at the time).

## Timing
- 14 Nov, desk work, plus two tests on a second machine: one RTX 3090 (250 W) and one RX 7900 XTX, both running a dense 27B Qwen model (4-bit) in the llama.cpp fork. The model's own MTP head at depth 2 is the baseline, because that is how the second machine serves it.
- Test 1: 3 coding prompts × 1,024 tokens, temperature 0 and 0.6.
- Test 2: 14 real competitive-programming problems with long reasoning (about 25K tokens each).

## Verdict
- **Neither drafter can run on DeepSeek V4.1 today, and neither looks worth building.** The DFlash drafter was trained for V4, not V4.1; it would need retraining on datacenter GPUs for weeks, and even then its acceptance matches DSpark's. That idea is closed.
- **DSpine's big wins come from long windows that are cheap to verify.** On a dense 27B model on a 3090, an extra verified token costs about 0.3 ms (~1 % of a step). On our engine it costs 2.7–2.9 ms, 15–27 % of a step (DR-16), so 15-ahead windows do not pay here (INFERRED). On the XTX, even the dense model lost at 8 ahead. Part of that may be the fork's ROCm path, not only the card (INFERRED).
- In real long reasoning, DSpine's acceptance at 3 ahead was 57 %. That is about what DSpark already keeps on our model (DR-16). The large case is not shown on real work.
- **What would change this:** someone training and releasing a V4.1 DSpine, or verify rows getting much cheaper (DR-16, DR-17). DR-16 measured the two inputs this model needed.

## Reproduce
- Not reproducible from the release. The tests used a public llama.cpp fork on another model; the desk models are not published.

## Notes / caveats
- All paper and test figures are reported, not re-run by us. Test 1 has three prompts. The second machine's tests are on a dense model on one GPU, so they bound what a drafter design can do; they do not predict our engine.
- While doing this we corrected one of our own notes: the deployed drafter is DSpark (`--dspark-block 3 --dspark-conf 0.3`).

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
