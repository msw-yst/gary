# FG-37: Where a served four-user decode step goes: 26.6 ms, the lead waits 3.5 ms for the MI50s, and their chain grows with the batch
Status: finding (measurement build not in the published release) · Date: 2026-11-14
Code: not in the fork's release commit (internal build `phasetime` ce66ecf4: the fusion-1 build plus the untraced phase stamps of FG-17). Flags: `DS4_PHASE_TIMES=1` (measurement only, default off); otherwise build 15's served flags, server parallel slots (`PAR`) 4.

## What and why
- FG-17 timed the phases of a one-row step in one process. Served traffic batches several users, and the MI50 helpers compute each token's experts per token. This run asked where a served four-user step goes, and whether the MI50 chain matters more under load.

## Prediction
- Where served time goes (no numeric prediction); the stamps' overhead ≤ 2 %. Pre-registered: yes (overhead bar only).

## Correctness
- Token-for-token identity of stamps on against off was not printed in this run's summary. The overhead check: 147.6 tok/s with stamps against 150.5 without (−1.9 %, one rep, inside the bar) (MEASURED).

## Timing
- 14 Nov 09:44 UTC, after the 13 Nov hardware change (HW-09): 4 × RX 7900 XTX + 4 × MI50 helpers at 135 W, DPM `high`. Served, four concurrent users, drafter on (served defaults), one rep; the four-user window has 250 steps.

## Verdict
- **The MI50 helper chain is on the critical path and matters more under load:** the MI50 kernels handle a window's rows one token at a time, so the chain grows with the batch, and the lead's wait for it grows from 11 % to 14 % of a step.
- The target this set: batching the MI50 gate/up and down across the tokens of a window. That became the persistent helper-grid and multi-token kernel work of 14–20 Nov (both later found neutral or worse served; see ME-17's table).
