# FG-31: Why the load-ahead attention kernel and side-stream routing are slower: the attention kernel is 27 µs a layer slower untraced; side routing publishes late
Status: explained (both dropped; flags default off, not merged) · Date: 2026-11-21
Code: not in the release commit of Gary, our fork of Strata (internal measurement branches only). Flags: `DS4_ATTN_PRE=1`; `DS4_LP_SIDE` bit 2 ROUTE, plus bit 4 ROUTE-SEQ (shared expert waits for the whole side routing), bit 8 ROUTE-LO (side routing on a lowest-priority stream), bit 16 ROUTE-GATE (shared expert waits only for the side's gate matvec); `DS4_STAGETIME` (GPU events at stage boundaries, measurement only); all default off.

## What and why
- Two bit-exact changes measured slower untraced: `DS4_ATTN_PRE` (+6 %, FG-29) and ROUTE (+8 %, FG-30). Under a kernel trace both looked free or faster. We wanted to know where the time goes, without a tracer, before writing either off.
- Method: GPU events at stage boundaries on one sampled layer a token (later 4 + 4 layers), on all 4 XTX and the lead's side stream; in-process ABBA so every arm shares one expert placement; then three ROUTE variants to separate contention, stream priority and the side stream's own cost.

## Prediction
- Host-headroom hypothesis (from FG-30's traced arms): the changes are slow because they eat the host's submission run-ahead. Refuted (FG-35).
- Third stage-timing run: that one of the ROUTE variants would remove the side-routing contention. No variant passed.

## Correctness
- Every run: arms identical to off on their token ids (EXACT PASS) (MEASURED).

## Timing
- 21 Nov, the test machine, 4 XTX + 5 MI50 helpers, the native MXFP4 pack, frozen placement, 1 user 2K, greedy, drafter off, untraced. Runs: a timing loop with one traced arm (03:21), the host-headroom run (05:11, FG-35), stage timing 1 (05:44), stage timing 2 (12:19; 4,000 tokens, 48 ABBA blocks, 4 + 4 sampled layers), stage timing 3 (13:27).

## Verdict
- **`DS4_ATTN_PRE` is settled: the load-ahead attention kernel itself is ~27 µs a layer slower untraced** (97 % of the delta in the better-sampled run), about +1.3 ms a token. Dropped; a rework would have to be of the kernel. Why the tracer hides it is still open (clocks or concurrency under the tracer).
- **ROUTE is explained:** moving routing to a side stream publishes the request to the MI50s later, so their answers arrive later and the lead waits at the join. As built it costs about +0.9 ms a token, consistent across runs once the freezes are removed. No variant fixes it: the side stream costs +5.9 µs by itself, and priority is honoured, so side routing cannot beat the main stream as built. Dropped.
- **JOIN costs +0.9 % with proper sampling;** the earlier +5.8–6.6 % were noise. Dropped as no gain.

## Reproduce
- Not reproducible from the release: the builds are internal.

## Notes / caveats
- **Correction (freezes):** Stage timing 3 first reported "off 32.97 ms a token, every variant ~22 ms" and a ~546 µs-a-layer stall in the lead's HC stage, and stage timing 2's ROUTE had one half at +25.9 ms. Both were a few multi-second whole-process freezes early in decode (e.g. one 1.57 s frozen layer, tokens of 7.2–9.4 s), not a per-layer stall and not arm-specific. With them removed, the walls are as in the table. The freeze cause was parked; later summarisers drop tokens over 400 ms or over 10× the median.
- One sampled layer a token in stage timing 1 gives large per-layer noise; stage timing 2's 8 sampled layers and 48 blocks are the better reading.
- The same runs measured a push all-reduce (`DS4_AR_PUSH`): its kernels cost ~93 µs a layer (+27 % untraced); its arithmetic is bit-exact window by window, and an earlier "not exact from token 6" was the build having it loaded, not using it. Not carded here.
