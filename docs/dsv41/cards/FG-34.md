# FG-34: Decode-attention launch fusion on release candidate r2a (`DS4_ATTN_FOLD`): two launches a layer folded into sparse attention; built, not run; worth about 1 % served
Status: not run (built; compile check cut; package staged) · Date: 2026-11-22
Code: not in the release commit of Gary, our fork of Strata (internal branch only, on release candidate r2a). Flags: `DS4_ATTN_FOLD` bit 1 (o finish inside sparse attention), bit 2 (second merged launch removed; needs `DS4_ATTN_MERGE`), `=3` both; `DS4_ATTN_FOLD_ABBA=B` (in-process A/B); default off. Engages only with `DS4_ATTN_SPLIT` off.

## What and why
- Graph replay cannot remove the ~3.8 µs gap of a dependent launch; removing the launch does (FG-32). This desk study listed every launch of r2a's decode-attention block in the served environment and built the fusions with the best saving for the risk.
- The launch list (from the graph-capture argument log, MEASURED; call order from the code, INFERRED): a plain layer (27–31 of 40) has **10 launches on every XTX** from the HC mix through the all-reduce; indexer and compressor layers have 16–23, Engram layers more. The same in a 1-row window and in a 2–6-row verify window: every launch takes the window's rows together.
- A removed dependent launch saves ~3.8 µs of GPU time (from two earlier merges: −0.461 ms over 120 launches, −0.157 ms over 40) (INFERRED from MEASURED results).

## Prediction
- Written before any run: exact; −0.16 to −0.31 ms a window (2 launches × 40 layers × 3.8 µs = −0.30, less +0.04–0.08 of per-row work moved onto the critical blocks; central −0.22). Drafter off that is −0.16 to −0.31 ms a token (0.8–1.5 % of 20.45). **Served, one user, it is −0.04 to −0.08 ms a token (0.5–1 % of 8.08)**, because the saving is per window and a served window yields ~3.85 tokens. In ms a token the drafted arms are predicted NEUTRAL by the 0.10 ms bar.

## Correctness
- Bit-exact by construction (INFERRED): each moved piece runs the same device code as before (the finish row and ring-row helpers that the merged launches already use), the attention body is a statement-for-statement copy of the current kernel, and nothing reorders a sum.
- A load check (`attn_fold_check`) compares every output byte for byte against the separate launches at windows of 1, 4 and 7 rows on every card, both unpack orders, with o's int8 rows on and off; any refusal fails the load. Not yet run.

## Verdict
- **Built and staged, not measured.** The fold removes 2 of 10 attention launches a layer, exactly by construction, with a byte-for-byte load check.
- **It is worth at most about 1 % served** (−0.04 to −0.08 ms a token). Launch overhead is paid once a window, and a served window yields ~3.85 tokens. This corrects our earlier graph-capture study, which priced launch fusion at −0.30 to −0.44 ms a token served; that figure holds per window, not per token.
- Low priority next to the CPU-lane levers (placement, ~1.5–2 ms a token) and a longer drafting block. Queue it only after those.
