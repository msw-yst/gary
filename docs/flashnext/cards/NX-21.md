# NX-21: MI50 DPM pinned high beats auto on the pool
Status: finding (we pin the MI50s at `high` while serving) · Date: 2026-11-22
Code: no engine change. Setting: `power_dpm_force_performance_level` of each MI50 (sysfs), `high` while the pool serves, back to `auto` when idle.

## What and why
- Every earlier pool cell ran with the MI50s pinned at DPM `high`: at `auto` gfx906 clocks down between bursty helper launches. Is the pin still worth it on the pool at full load, where the helpers are busy every layer?

## Prediction
- Pre-registered: yes. auto 2-6 % slower per agent (ESTIMATE). Bar: keep the pin if auto < 0.98x high per agent.

## Correctness
- The 16-user speed-prompt texts are identical in all four cells and equal to [NX-20](NX-20.md)'s (MEASURED).

## Timing
- Hardware: 2 × RX 7900 XTX 24 GB (272 W cap), 4 × Instinct MI50 16 GB (135 W cap), EPYC 7642 (48 cores), 256 GB DDR4-2666.
- Workload: pool on hugetlbfs, 8 slots per worker, MTP off, 8+8 users × 384 tokens, temperature 0; order auto, high, high, auto. Each cell's status line confirmed the DPM level.

## Results
| DPM | per agent (cells) | slowest | aggregate | MI50 board power under load | label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| auto | 17.71, 17.96 | 15.88, 16.58 | 250.1, 255.7 | 75-82 W | MEASURED |
| high | 18.36, 18.57 | 16.09, 16.29 | 256.8, 261.0 | 82-94 W | MEASURED |

auto / high: 0.966 per agent, 0.980 aggregate (MEASURED).

## Verdict
- Keep the pin while serving: auto is 3.4 % slower per agent, below the 0.98 bar; both auto cells are below both high cells. The pin costs ~9 W per MI50 under load (~36 W for four).

## Reproduce
- `echo high > /sys/class/drm/card<N>/device/power_dpm_force_performance_level` for each MI50 after the workers have loaded; `auto` again when they stop.

## Notes / caveats
- Measured at 8+8 only; at lighter loads the helpers are idler and the clock ramp matters more or less, not measured.
- Absolute numbers come from a private prompt set (8 prompts, not published); ratios between arms of the same run are the comparable quantity.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output), ESTIMATE (our arithmetic) or INFERRED (our own reading).
