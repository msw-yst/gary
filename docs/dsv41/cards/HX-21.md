# HX-21: Lead-path A/B: taking the lead XTX off the MoE all-reduce's critical path
Status: kept (in part: PUBFUSE, JOINCOMB, FLAGFUSE in build 12; ROUTE dropped) · Date: 2026-11-11
Code: in the fork's release commit. Flags: `DS4_AHEAD=0` (existing switch), `DS4_LP_ROUTE=1`, `DS4_LP_PUBFUSE=1`, `DS4_LP_JOINCOMB=1`, `DS4_LP_FLAGFUSE=1` (all default off).

## What and why
- HX-20 showed the lead XTX (XTX #1) arriving last at every MoE all-reduce, with the three members waiting about 1.0 ms a token for it. A re-cut of the same timeline put the lead-only work at 32.5 µs a layer: 16.5 µs of join (waiting for the latest MI50 with a pick) and 16.0 µs of other lead-only work.
- Five changes, each aimed at a named part of that:
  - `DS4_AHEAD=0`: drop the lookahead router GEMV paired into each layer's router (6.0 µs a layer in an older trace);
  - `DS4_LP_ROUTE=1`: pick the top-6 experts per warp and merge once, 3 barriers instead of 13 (the routing kernel was 14.1 µs traced);
  - `DS4_LP_PUBFUSE=1`: publish the one-row helper request from inside the routing launch (−1 dependent launch a layer);
  - `DS4_LP_JOINCOMB=1`: the lead's join and its combine in one launch;
  - `DS4_LP_FLAGFUSE=1` (MI50 side): the helper's done flag written by the last block of its combine, not by a separate kernel after its graph.
- A desk analysis written before the run (ANATOMY §5) expected `DS4_AHEAD=0` to do nothing on build 11: with the BF16 router the paired lookahead kernel is not launched.

## Verdict
- **Only the MI50-side change paid in full.** FLAGFUSE saved 0.20 ms, inside its prediction, and the timestamps show why: the lead's join wait fell from 19.5 to 13.2 µs a layer (median 15.2 → 8.8), because the helpers' results arrive earlier.
- **`DS4_AHEAD=0` did nothing (+0.02)**, as the desk analysis said and against the run script's −0.15 to −0.30. The lookahead GEMV it removes is not launched with the BF16 router. That item is struck from the lead-only list.
- **The routing rewrite did nothing (+0.01)**, and its pre-registered disproof fired twice: RT − AH0 > −0.15, and the release → publish segment stayed at 49.0 µs against ~33 predicted. The 14.1 µs routing kernel came from a traced run; untraced, the kernel's in-situ cost is evidently not where the prediction put it [I]. Dropped.
- **PUBFUSE gave −0.11 against −0.25 to −0.40** (most of that range assumed AH0 would pay). JOINCOMB gave ~0 against −0.05 to −0.15.
- **Why the lead-side cuts did not show [I]:** in the ~27 layers a token where a helper has a pick, the lead reaches its join before the latest MI50 is done. A launch saved on the lead before or at the join (JOINCOMB's launch sits in front of the spin) only makes the lead start waiting sooner: the time moves into the helper wait. Only changes that make the helpers earlier (FLAGFUSE) or that act after the join pay one for one. The join in the ALL timeline (13.9 µs) is no shorter than with FLAGFUSE alone (13.2), which fits that reading.
- **ALL: −0.27 ms against −0.55 to −1.00.** Disproved (Δ > −0.40). The member margin fell only from 35.7 to 29.7 µs, against ~19 predicted. The lead is still last.
- PUBFUSE, JOINCOMB and FLAGFUSE (with `DS4_AHEAD=0`, harmless) went into the build-12 stack (SV-09), where together with the fusions they added −0.23 ms over the fusions alone.
- **B8 is unusable from this run**: the two base runs drifted from 44.5 to 52.2 ms a step, larger than any arm's effect. No B8 claim is made.

## Notes / caveats
- One run an arm for B1. The rep spread of B1 on this setup is about 0.04 ms (FG-07, SV-09), so −0.10 is resolvable but −0.01 is not.
- Under the timestamp build, PUBFUSE keeps the stamped publish and JOINCOMB stands aside, so neither has a slice measurement; only their ms.
- The "moved into the helper wait" explanation is our reading of the critical-path model and the join timestamps. No arm isolates it directly.
