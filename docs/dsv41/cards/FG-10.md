# FG-10: Indexer top-k over many workgroups (`DS4_TOPK_MB`): the long-context decode cost
Status: kept (build 13; served) · Date: 2026-11-12
Code: in Gary's release commit. Flags: `DS4_TOPK_MB=1` (default off; on in the served environment from build 13).

## What and why
- A decode-time budget of build 12 from 1K to 110K tokens of context found that almost all of the per-token growth (+0.68 ms, predicted +0.65) sat in the sparse-attention indexer, 0.54 → 1.21 ms a token (REPORTED from the run notes). The indexer scores every compressed key and then picks the top 512; the pick ran in one workgroup.
- `DS4_TOPK_MB` splits that top-k over up to 64 workgroups when there are at least 8,192 scores, and merges. Below that it keeps the old kernel.

## Prediction
- ms a token, on − off: 110K −0.45 [−0.65, −0.20] (primary); 64K −0.25 [−0.40, −0.08]; 32K −0.12 [−0.25, +0.03]; 1K +0.00 [−0.05, +0.05] (guard: must not change). Pre-registered: yes, in the run's prereg, before the run.

## Correctness
- 4 identity lines IDENTICAL, with engagement checked: unset vs the base build; on vs off at 32K and 110K (one row) and at 32K with 3 rows (MEASURED). The selection is the same set of 512 keys by construction, so the attention input is unchanged.

## Verdict
- **−0.39 ms a token at 110K and −0.85 ms at 256K, exact, nothing at 1K.** Kept, on in the served environment from build 13. It is the largest single lever for long-context decode so far.
- The model behind the prediction was wrong in shape: the old top-k cost 60–80 µs a call, roughly flat in the number of scores (latency-bound), not 6 µs plus 1.6 ns a key as modelled. The end result still fell inside the predicted range (INFERRED from the load-time timing).
- Batched decode uses another path; FG-11 applies the same idea there.
