# QA-22: Community low-bit packs: how they fit, why none loaded as published, and a loader that mixes low-bit gate/up with an MXFP4 down (exact on the served pack)
Status: finding (fit study + loader on an internal build; the loader is exact on the served pack) · Date: 2026-11-22
Code: not in the fork's release commit (internal build `mixload-r2`, commit ecb1e5aa, on top of release candidate r2a `stack-r2a-rc1` 8c7aa75e). Flags: none (the loader picks each matrix's kernel by its type).

## What and why
- The native MXFP4 experts (268.95 GiB) do not fit our GPUs, so about 4,200 experts run on the CPU lane, the slowest part of a token. Community GGUF packs of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 2–3 bits are smaller; a community report (single runs, graded by the 3-bit model itself) said a 3-bit pack did agent tasks well where a 2-bit one did not (REPORTED).
- Before downloading anything: how big are the published low-bit packs' experts, would they fit, would our engine load them, and what does conversion need?
- Packs studied (unsloth's published GGUFs): UD-IQ3_S, UD-IQ3_XXS, UD-Q2_K_XL, UD-Q4_K_XL.

## Prediction
- Earlier estimates: a full IQ3_XXS pack "~199 GiB of experts against ~203 GiB of room" (20 Nov); UD-IQ3_S "~220 GiB" (21 Nov). Not pre-registered.

## Correctness
- The MXFP4 parts of these packs are byte-identical to our served experts: 7 of 7 sampled 1 MiB slices and 3 of 3 64 KiB samples matched by sha256 (MEASURED). So letting one matrix of a layer stay MXFP4 is exact, not a requantisation.
- Loader, CPU unit test: all 257 type triples the old rule accepted keep the same route; 16 newly accepted triples, all a low-bit gate == up beside an MXFP4 down; every matrix of a synthetic mixed layer bit-identical to its own type's reference dot product (MEASURED).
- Loader on the served native pack, teacher-forced on 12 prompts: **EXACT** against r2a at all 2,900 positions (MEASURED, QA-23's gate).

## Timing
- 21–22 Nov. Fit: GGUF headers and tensor tables read by HTTP range requests (about 38 MB in all; nothing downloaded). Room: the placement logs of r2a's served layout (4 XTX, 5 MI50 helpers). Not a timing run.

- Room for experts: 195.7 GiB (configuration A) / 193.7 GiB (configuration B; the two served configurations of r2a's release gate) (MEASURED from placement logs).
- **Why none loaded as published:** in many layers the gate and up are low-bit while the down stays MXFP4 (UD-IQ3_S 18 of 40 layers, UD-IQ3_XXS 30, UD-Q2_K_XL 7), and our loader refused to mix MXFP4 with other types in one layer. UD-Q2_K_XL's Q5_K Engram table is also a format our Engram reader does not handle (MEASURED from the tables and the source).
- The published packs are not uniform: UD-IQ3_S's experts are 141 GiB MXFP4, 47 GiB Q3_K, 32 GiB IQ3_XXS, 14 GiB IQ2_S and 2 GiB IQ3_S; UD-IQ3_XXS's are 32 % MXFP4 and 24 % Q3_K (MEASURED).
- A uniform IQ3_XXS pack (hypothetical) would need 193.8 GiB: it fits in configuration A by about 146 experts (0.9 %) and misses in B by about 14 (INFERRED from the sizes).
- Only the low-bit tensors need fetching, since the MXFP4 ones are our own bytes: UD-IQ3_S 101.7 GB (57 tensors), UD-IQ3_XXS 152.1 GB (90 tensors) (MEASURED); a test fetch ran at 96.6 MB/s.

## Verdict
- **The loader now accepts a low-bit gate / up beside an MXFP4 down**: each matrix goes to its own kernel family (the low-bit gate / up to the i-quant kernels, the down to the same MXFP4 kernel an all-MXFP4 layer uses), on the XTX, the MI50 helpers and the CPU lane. Served packs take exactly the old paths, and the GPU check confirms it bit for bit.
- Conversion needs no new pack format: our packer overrides the routed experts on top of the served pack, so dense weights, Engram and the drafter stay byte-identical, and only the experts change.
- The 20–21 Nov estimates were off: the published UD-IQ3_XXS needs 208.9 GiB, not ~199, and does not fit; UD-IQ3_S is 236 GiB, not ~220. Only UD-Q2_K_XL fits whole.
- The quality of the 3-bit IQ3_S pack: QA-23. Speed of any of these packs: not measured.

## Reproduce
- Read a GGUF's header and tensor table with HTTP range requests and sum the routed-expert tensors by type. The loader change is not in the published release.

## Notes / caveats
- Lane-wait predictions assume the CPU lane stays bandwidth-bound; Q3_K has no tuned CPU rows, and in mixed layers the lane computes the MXFP4 down with a generic single-row dot product, so the real lane time may be higher (INFERRED, unmeasured).
- Packs with a 256-value down type change how expert rows split across the four XTX (3/2/2/2 blocks instead of 5/5/4/4), a possible extra XTX cost of about 20 % on the busiest card's expert work (INFERRED).
- The XTX prompt tiles do not cover Q3_K, so prompt reads with these packs may be slower (INFERRED).

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).

Sources: no published data extract for this card; the fit and loader write-ups of 21–22 Nov; udq12-20261122T044903Z (exactness gate); the run summaries are held privately.
