# FG-15: Where a decode step's time goes on build 15: a fixed 11 ms a step, and a kernel trace
Status: finding · Date: 2026-11-13
Code: in Gary's release commit (build 15, served flags, drafter off). Flags: none new; the engine's batch test and a profiler trace of one-row decode.

## What and why
- Before choosing the next decode work, two questions: how much of a batched decode step is a fixed cost shared by every user, and, for one row, where on the lead XTX that time goes.
- **Part 1:** the batch test at B = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 (two passes), fitted as step = F + u × B, plus 16 solo references and two untraced one-row timelines (64-token prompt).
- **Part 2:** a kernel trace of the same one-row decode, read for each stage's share of the layer part. The tracer inflates time, so shares are the comparable quantity, and milliseconds are rescaled to the untraced step [I].

## Prediction
- Part 1: F 10.9 ms (10–11.6), u 3.63 ms a user (3.3–4.0), r² ≥ 0.99; the untraced one-row step 13 ms (12.5–13.6); every B inside its band; a batch-path tax at B1 of 0.45 ms (0.15–0.8). An earlier estimate put F at 10.6 ms. Pre-registered: yes, before the run.
- Part 2: shares of the layer part: attention blocks 49 % (42–56), publish 11 % (8–15), the wait from publish to the join 18 % (13–24), join + combine 10 % (6–15), all-reduces 12 % (6–17); 1,080 launches a token (950–1,250). Pre-registered: yes.

## Correctness
- Every batched sequence equalled its solo reference (MEASURED, fixed-cost.txt).

## Timing
- 13 Nov 22:51, after the hardware change (HW-09): 4 × RX 7900 XTX + 4 × MI50 at 135 W, build 15. The trace's summary failed to write at first (a run-directory permission fault); the captured trace was re-analysed afterwards from a copy, with no re-run.

## Verdict
- **Part 1 PASS.** 88 % of a one-row step is a fixed cost every user shares (F 11.07 ms; the earlier 10.6 ms estimate is confirmed); each user adds 3.46 ms. The host has finished launching after ~3.7 ms and then waits ~8.7 ms on the GPUs, so host launch overhead is not the limit at B1 [I].
- **Part 2 PASS:** every predicted share landed in its band. The largest parts are the attention blocks (45 %) and the stretch between the lead's publish and the helper join (22 %), which holds the lead's own expert chain as well as the MI50 wait.
- **The gaps are real (corrected 14 Nov).** A follow-up desk study (FG-16) first read the 3.07 ms of "inter-kernel gaps" as mostly a tracer artefact, with a real gap of about 0.1–1.1 µs a boundary [I]. Untraced on-device timestamps then measured the real gap at about 2.9 µs a boundary (FG-17, MEASURED there), against the trace's 3.6–3.8 µs: about 1,070 kernels × ~2.9 µs ≈ 3.1 ms a token is gap time [I], so fewer launches could be worth up to about 3 ms a token. The fusion plan's five items are worth about 3–4 % of that step (FG-16).

## Reproduce

## Notes / caveats
- The trace's milliseconds are a proportional rescale of traced shares (INFERRED). A later build with untraced on-device phase stamps measured the same split without a tracer (FG-17; not in this release): attention 5.61 ms, publish → join 2.67, router + publish 1.40, join + combine 0.90, the two all-reduces 0.77 and 0.78 of a 12.97 ms step, close to this card's rescaled shares.
- The B8 point sits slightly below the fit's line in some earlier runs; a fit on B ≤ 4 gives F 11.13, u 3.43.

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
