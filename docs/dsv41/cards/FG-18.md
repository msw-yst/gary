# FG-18: Where the MI50 helper's time goes in a served step, and whether graph replay removes the XTX launch gap
Status: finding (measurement build not in the published release) · Date: 2026-11-15
Code: not in the published release; the code is not in the public commit. Build 17 plus the untraced phase stamps of FG-17 (`DS4_PHASE_TIMES=1`, default off), and two side probes: a launch-gap probe (stream vs graph replay) and an XTX → MI50 peer-flag probe.

## What and why
- FG-17 measured, in one process, that the MI50 helpers finish after the lead's join in 98 % of layers. This run asks how big that wait is when served, and which of three levers is worth building:
  - A: graph replay on the XTXs, if it saves ≥ 1.4 µs a kernel;
  - B: a faster helper head (its PCIe reads), if the served one-user ceiling is ≥ 2 % of a step;
  - C: peer flags from XTX to MI50, if their latency is ≤ 3 µs.

## Prediction
- Single process, one row, drafter off, per token: attention 5.36 ms, its all-reduce 0.85, router and publish 1.50, the lead's own experts 2.60, join and combine 1.00, the MoE all-reduce 0.57, step 12.62 ms (each with a range). Real gap between kernels 0.5 µs (0.1–1.5); helper chain 65 µs a window (45–80); helper later than the join in 55 % of layers (35–75). Pre-registered: yes, before the run.

## Correctness
- Stamps on vs off: identical tokens on the single-process run, a second pair and the drafter-on run (MEASURED). Stamp overhead +0.58 % a token (MEASURED).

## Timing
- 15 Nov, 4 × RX 7900 XTX + 4 × MI50 at 135 W. Single process (one row, drafter off and on) and served one user (4 slots), then the two side probes.

## Verdict
- **Lever A is out.** Graph replay saves 0.4–0.9 µs a kernel on the XTXs, not 1.4; the real gap stays about 2.9 µs.
- **Lever B is on.** Served, the lead waits for the helpers 9–13 % of a one-user step, much more than one process suggests (0.50 ms of 12.6). A faster helper head is the next build (`DS4_HSTEP_HEAD`, not yet measured).
- **Lever C is inconclusive.** The probe's ack path needs fixing first.
