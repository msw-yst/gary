# HX-33: MI50 expert kernels against llama.cpp and castagna-veloce (QBENCH): ours fastest for 2-bit, 1.5× slower for MXFP4 at batch 1
Status: finding · Date: 2026-11-16
Code: in Gary's release commit (release 3). The bench is `ds4_iq_helper_bench --pool-file` (`tests/dsv41/iq_helper_bench.cpp`); the reference harness and the run scripts are in `tools/qbench/`. No engine switch is involved: the bench calls the helper's expert path directly.

## What and why
- The MI50s serve routed experts for the XTX group (the helpers). Before investing in placement or a native-MXFP4 build, we wanted to know whether our gfx906 expert kernels are near what the card can do, measured against the two other gfx906 expert paths we know of: upstream llama.cpp's MMVQ / MMQ kernels and castagna-veloce's coalesced and fused MoE kernels.
- One routed DeepSeek V4.1 Flash expert (n_embd 5120, n_ff 2304: gate, up, SwiGLU, down), applied to B rows that all picked it.

## Prediction
- Pre-registered before any run: MI50 within ±30 % of the XTX for IQ2 at batch 1 (or 1.6–2.5× slower); MI50 MXFP4 more than 2× our IQ2_XS, at 80–150 µs; castagna at least 1.5× our MI50 GB/s; our error rel_rms ≤ 1e-2, rel_max ≤ 5e-2, nothing non-finite; the helper round trip 90–130 µs (HX-34).

## Correctness
- **Ours:** every cell's combined output was compared with a CPU fp32 reference (weights decoded by ggml's CPU code, fp32 activations, double-precision sums, SwiGLU limit 10): rel_max 1.0–1.3 %, rel_rms 1.12–1.21 %, no non-finite value, no launch error (MEASURED). The rel_rms is the same for every format, so it is the int8 rounding of x and h, not the weight format (INFERRED); it misses the pre-registered 1e-2 narrowly.
- **castagna-veloce and llama.cpp:** only checked for non-finite outputs (none in any cell). Their numerical error was not measured.

## Timing
- 16 Nov 14:11–14:19 UTC, one Instinct MI50 32 GB (gfx906, 60 CUs, 135 W cap) in the 4 XTX + 4 MI50 test machine, after its 16 Nov rebuild; nothing else running; MI50 peak 47 °C. ROCm 7.14 on Linux.
- **Data:** real expert bytes, unmodified, experts 0–39 of one layer per format: IQ2_XXS, IQ2_XS and IQ2_S from the 2.25 bpw IQ2 pack this engine serves, MXFP4 from a native-MXFP4 GGUF of the same model. The 40 experts rotate per call, so every read is cold. Q2_K/Q3_K cells use legal random blocks (no Q2_K/Q3_K pack of this model was available).
- **GPU µs:** rocprofv3 kernel trace, sum of kernel durations per expert (ours: gate_up + down + the three small int8 / combine launches; the references: their quantise and matrix kernels). The like-for-like number: launch gaps excluded.
- **Wall µs:** ours = p50 of 300 calls queued back to back (event-timed, no profiler); references = p50 of graph time / 40 experts, 200 repetitions.
- **Versions:** ours = this release; llama.cpp b11870 (74bca395), built for gfx906; castagna-veloce `main` at 3483d4627, ggml targets built for gfx906 (cleanly).
- B is the number of rows that picked the expert. "win" is the helper's window path (all rows in one pass); "tok" is the lead's one-token path.

## Verdict
- **For 2-bit experts our MI50 kernels are the fastest of the three at every batch measured.** At batch 1: IQ2_XXS 51.6 against 70.2 (castagna) and 79.6 (llama.cpp); IQ2_XS 53.1 / 62.3 / 81.1; IQ2_S 56.2 / 134.1 / 91.0.
- **For MXFP4 at batch 1–2 we are about 1.5× slower** (80.8 against 54.2 and 58.5 µs). Level at batch 4, ahead at 8. A tuned gfx906 MXFP4 kernel could save about 25 µs an expert (INFERRED from the references).
- **The fastest expert path on the MI50 is castagna-veloce's fused Q2_K/Q3_K MoE kernel:** 29.1 µs per expert-equivalent, 43 % of peak, against 17–19 % for our best IQ2 cells. It is a different quantisation and a whole-layer fusion, so it shows what an aligned, fused gfx906 kernel reaches, not what our IQ2 kernels should do per expert.
- Predictions: MI50 within ±30 % of the XTX for IQ2 at batch 1 held (51.6–56.2 against 47.3–52.6 µs GPU, FG-19); MXFP4 80–150 µs held (80.8); MXFP4 more than 2× our IQ2_XS did not (1.5×); castagna ≥ 1.5× our GB/s did not hold for single IQ2 experts (castagna is slower) and was borderline for MXFP4 (1.49×); error rel_rms ≤ 1e-2 missed narrowly (1.1–1.2e-2), rel_max held.
- No path is near the MI50's peak for one expert at decode; only many experts per launch get close.

## Reproduce
- `tools/qbench/README.md`: build the two bench targets with `tools/dsv41/build.sh`, the references with `tools/qbench/build-ref.sh`, the expert pools with `tools/qbench/mkpool.py`, then `tools/qbench/run-card.sh` on one MI50 (phases ours, lcpp, cv, kt).

## Notes / caveats
- One MI50, one run. The kernel-trace numbers are means over 100 (ours) or 50 × 40 (references) calls; repeat runs were not made.
- The bench measures one live pick per call. The engine's one-token path launches all of a token's picks together, so the bench overstates our fixed cost per expert by up to the ~11 µs of small launches (INFERRED).
- castagna-veloce's IQ2_S path is much slower than its IQ2_XXS / IQ2_XS paths here; we did not investigate why.
- The gfx906 expert kernels timed here are unchanged since release 2 (UPDATE-2026-11-16.md).

Numbers are labelled MEASURED (read from a raw run file or a script-written summary), REPORTED (from a commit message or write-up at the time, not re-checked against raw output) or INFERRED (our own reading).
