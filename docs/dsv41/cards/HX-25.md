# HX-25: MI50 multi-row levers: a fused down kernel and multi-row kernels, both slower
Status: dropped (the MI50 multi-row kernel levers are spent) · Date: 2026-11-12
Code: not in the fork's release commit (internal branch only). Flags: `DS4_G906_FUSEDOWN=1` (+ `_TOK`); multi-row arms MR, RL, RLA (bench only).

## What and why
- With the drafter on, most MI50 helper windows carry 2–4 rows (HX-24, FG-08). Two ideas targeted those windows:
  - **Fused down:** the MI50 window's int8 rounding of the hidden rows, the down projection and the combine in one kernel instead of three.
  - **Multi-row kernels:** kernels that load each weight block once for all rows of the window (MR), plus two row-loop variants (RL, RLA).

## Prediction
- Fused down: a win at one row of at least −3 µs a window, with no regression at 2–4 rows (each cell ≤ +2.0 µs).
- Multi-row: 2–4-row windows −1.5 µs (MR) or −5.0 µs (RL, RLA) on average, no cell worse than +2.0 µs (+4.0 at 6 experts). Pre-registered: yes, for both, before the runs.

## Correctness
- The fused-down arms and MR were bit-identical to the old kernels in every cell, with engagement confirmed. The row-loop arm RL was "bit-identical or within tolerance" (largest relative difference 1.34e-5), so it was not exact (MEASURED from the bench summaries; not in the extract, which carries the timing verdicts).
- The fused-down build first tripped the code-object check: its fat binary grew by 1.38 MB. All of the growth was in the MI50 code; every XTX code object was unchanged, so the check was refined to accept MI50-only growth when every XTX bundle is unchanged (QA-08).

## Timing
- Kernel benches on one MI50, two passes each, 40 cells (fused down) and the full multi-row cell set. No engine A/B was run, because neither passed its bench gate.

## Verdict
- **No winner in either bench.** The fused down kernel is slower at one row and far slower at 2–4 rows; MR is too small a gain to clear its line; RL and RLA are much slower. With HX-23 and HX-24, the MI50 kernel levers for the decode window are spent for now.
- No engine run was needed: the bench gate did its job in minutes on one card.

## Reproduce

## Notes / caveats
- These are cold-weight kernel benches; the absolute µs are larger than in-engine windows.
